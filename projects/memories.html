Memories of the Sent-Down Youth

Xishuangbanna, Yunnan

South of the South

18 minutes

Beneath the southern sky, memories stick to the rubber trees.在南方的天幕之下，記憶附着在橡膠樹上。

Before leaving Xishuangbanna, I went to the Fourth Branch of Jinghong Farm. On the hills behind it, rubber trees covered the slopes, all leaning to one side. Winding tubes and collection cups hung from their trunks. There was no telling how long they had been there. White sap seeped from cuts in the bark, some of it already dry. Decades ago, Grandma and Grandpa were sent here. On these rubber trees, they left countless knife marks, their sweat and their tears.

Grandma and Grandpa were my age when they worked here, in the Tenth Production Team. On the flight home, I gazed down at the mountains divided into patches, and the earth-coloured rivers. I remembered the first night I had begged Grandma to tell me stories about her years as a sent-down youth. I imagined her leaving, the jolting journeys. Had the slow trains, trucks and tractors that carried her passed through the places I was now flying over?

A few months earlier, Grandma and Grandpa had gone to Xishuangbanna for the winter, hoping to revisit those years. They called me from there. As we talked, they mentioned that the rubber factory had been torn down, along with the thatched dormitories and the canteen that served only rice. Ping An insurance company had bought the land and built luxury villas.

Looking at the photos they sent, I suddenly felt a sense of urgency, as though something I had never had was disappearing. So I hurriedly made plans to go to Xishuangbanna, to see this place before everything was covered over by something new, and to give the stories I had imagined from Grandma and Grandpa’s memories something I could see and touch.
