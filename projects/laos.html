A Cultural Exchange Trip in Laos

Luang Prabang · Vientiane

A Cultural Exchange Trip in Laos

From the ancient Buddhist traditions of Luang Prabang to the sound of students reading at Virathavone School in the capital, Vientiane, young people from China and Laos approached each other’s cultures and everyday lives with sincerity. With every conversation, a friendship that crossed national, linguistic and cultural boundaries quietly grew.

Luang Prabang, the former royal capital of Laos, is a place where layers of history meet modern life. Here, we began to discover the country’s Buddhist traditions and local ways of life. The thousand-year-old alms-giving ceremony began in the stillness of early morning, as barefoot monks walked through the morning dew, carrying a spirit of compassion and kindness. In the afternoon, the turquoise waters of Kuang Si Falls flowed gently, their cool mist offering relief from the summer heat. From the summit of Mount Phousi, we could see the entire city, while the setting sun cast a golden glow over the Mekong River. Three days later, we travelled to Vientiane on the China–Laos Railway linking Kunming and Vientiane.

For our cultural exchange sessions, we prepared activities for our Lao partners, with whom we had been paired one to one. These included paper cutting, Chinese knotting, Chinese folk songs, kung fu and an introduction to the sporting culture at Qibao Dwight. In return, the Lao students shared their culture through Lao language lessons and displays of traditional clothing. Together, we learned to make “Makbang,” intricate floral towers used for blessings, from banana leaves and marigolds. We also tried traditional Lao cooking in the school cafeteria and learned traditional Lao songs and dances in the music classroom. Outside class, students from both schools explored Vientiane’s cultural and religious landmarks together.

As our visit drew to a close, our Lao friends joined us for a traditional Baci ceremony. We sat together around a large golden Makbeng blessing tower as soft chanting filled the air. We tied cotton strings around one another’s wrists, exchanging our most heartfelt blessings, and the tears we had been holding back at the thought of leaving finally began to flow.

“The visit by Qibao Dwight’s students and teachers received considerable attention and high praise from the Lao government and people across different sectors of society. Ms Sounthone Xayachack, a member of the Secretariat of the Lao People’s Revolutionary Party Central Committee and Vice President of the National Assembly, warmly received our group. She expressed her hope that the visit would open the way for deeper exchange and cooperation between the two schools and between China and Laos. Ms Daravone Kittiphanh, Deputy Minister of Education and Sports of Laos; Ms Mo Xiaoling, Head of China Media Group’s Laos Bureau; and Ms Phoukham Vongvichit, Chairwoman of the Lancang-Mekong Development Foundation, also made a special appearance at the welcoming ceremony.”
